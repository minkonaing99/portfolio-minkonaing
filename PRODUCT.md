# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

- Recruiters and hiring managers evaluating Min Ko Naing for backend developer roles (Bangkok or remote). They skim fast; they need role, stack, proof of shipped work, and a contact path within seconds.
- Freelance clients (small businesses, teams needing web apps, dashboards, AI integrations). They need to trust that projects get delivered and maintained.
- Fellow developers browsing from GitHub or LinkedIn. They judge craft: if the portfolio itself is sloppy, the claim of "reliable backend work" fails.

Desired outcome: visitor contacts Min via email, LinkedIn, or messaging channels, or requests the CV.

## Product Purpose

Personal portfolio of Min Ko Naing, backend developer in Bangkok with a BSc in Computer Networks, preparing for an MSc in Cybersecurity & Information Assurance at Mahidol University. Showcases 17+ shipped projects (PHP/Laravel, Python, JS/React, SQL, AWS), work experience, and certificates. The site is itself a work sample: it must demonstrate the engineering values it claims.

## Brand Personality

Precise engineer. Quiet, structured, systems-minded. Confidence through restraint, not volume. The interface should read like well-built infrastructure: every element deliberate, nothing decorative for decoration's sake. Tone of copy: direct, concrete, measured; states what was built and what it achieved, no hype adjectives.

## Anti-references

- The template dev portfolio: generic hero + wall of skill icons + identical project card grid + "Let's create something amazing together" copy.
- Hacker cliché (matrix green, glitch text, neon cyber) even as the cybersecurity direction grows.
- Gradient-blob SaaS startup look, glassmorphism.
- Anything that looks AI-generated or theme-bought.

## Design Principles

1. The site is the proof. Craft in the portfolio itself is the strongest claim of engineering quality; sloppy details contradict the pitch.
2. Evidence over adjectives. Show shipped projects, measurable outcomes, real dates; cut self-praise copy.
3. Fast path to contact. Every audience ends at "get in touch"; contact must never be more than one obvious action away.
4. Restraint as signal. Fewer, more deliberate elements; whitespace and typography do the talking, not effects.
5. Vanilla and fast. No frameworks, minimal dependencies, quick load on mobile; performance is part of the brand.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard good practice: WCAG AA contrast, full keyboard navigability, visible focus states, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected, semantic HTML, alt text on all imagery. English-language audience, international (non-native) readers: plain sentences over idiom.
